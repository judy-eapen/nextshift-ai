# NextShift AI — 5-minute demo script (final, 2026-08-31)

*Record at http://localhost:8599 (`streamlit run ui/app.py`). Everything is pre-warmed: the ~23 popular careers have cached narrative summaries (instant), and the PM composite has run once. Sidebar → "Simulate a source outage" is the failure demo if you want it. Speak the bold lines; the rest is what to click. Timings assume brisk clicking — practice the click path once first.*

| Time | On screen | Say |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 | Start screen | **"Students only know a few dozen careers — you can't be interested in a job you've never heard of. And nobody who talks about AI and work says how confident they are or what to do next. NextShift does both: a Career Explorer over every occupation the U.S. government tracks, and an agent that plans with receipts — it never promises a job is safe, and it never invents a number."** |
| 0:25 | Click **Explore careers →**. Sweep the home page: search box, 7 traits, collections, 16 families | **"A thousand and seventeen real careers, browsable instantly — no AI call, everything from official data: O\*NET, BLS projections, the Anthropic Economic Index. Browse by what you like doing, by school subject, or by collections like 'careers you may not know exist'."** |
| 0:45 | Search **doctor** → point at the banner and the five cards | **"Search understands plain words and tells you when a word is bigger than five results — thirty kinds of doctor here, one click to the whole health family. Each card: real outlook, real AI-use data, and why it matched."** |
| 1:05 | Search **i am good at math** → open **Statisticians** | **"Or just say what you're good at."** Point at the four stat tiles. **"Four separate readings — 31,300 jobs today; projected up 11 percent, three times the average; AI already in 5 of its 19 tasks; and what to start building. Growth and AI exposure are deliberately separate numbers — a career can be both AI-heavy and growing."** |
| 1:30 | Scroll to **At a glance** | **"Then the part I like most: a model retells those facts in plain language — 'about 1,900 openings a year… suggests steady opportunity' — but it's grounded: it only ever sees the sourced facts on this page, every sentence is cited internally, fixed rules strip any number that isn't on a source card or any 'perfect career for you' language, and a second model from a different family reviews the rest. Here it removed 4 lines — listed under Review details. Generated once, cached for everyone."** |
| 2:05 | Click **☆ Save**, react **😀** on Statisticians; back-arrow in header → save 2 more (e.g. Mathematicians, a health career) → the banner appears → click **Yes — help me →** | **"Save a few, react, and it offers help. Watch what it does with the saved careers: they enter the interview as things I *noticed*, not things I chose."** |
| 2:30 | First seeded question appears (e.g. "You saved X and Y — visual creation, or analytical problems?"). Answer 1–2 questions live, then **Recommend careers now** → understanding gate | **"The interview asks comparison questions built from the saved careers' real profiles. A fixed rule picks each topic — the model only words and reads answers. And before a single lookup runs: gate one — here's what it understood, in my own words. I can edit or stop; stopping writes nothing."** |
| 3:00 | Confirm → (cut / timelapse the working panel) → career directions appear | **"Now it gathers evidence in parallel — BLS projections, task-level AI use, forecasts — and proposes directions where every 'why it fits you' line quotes what I actually said. Reviewer checked, removals listed."** |
| 3:25 | (Pre-recorded or cut) shortlist → deep dive → **Test this career** section → save gate → approve | **"Reactions narrow a shortlist, a deep dive ends with cheap ways to test the career before committing, and nothing is saved until I approve — gate two. Next visit, it shows what changed."** |
| 3:55 | *Start over* → **Start planning** (professional) → type **Product Manager**, paste the week, tick concerns → understanding gate with the composite task list | **"Same engine for professionals — with my favorite piece of honesty: the U.S. taxonomy has no 'Product Manager'. So NextShift assembles one from 23 real task statements across official occupations, shows its work, and labels every proxy figure. I untick what isn't my week."** |
| 4:25 | (Cut to a finished plan) direct answer → outlook → 30-day plan → *How we reached this answer* | **"The plan leads with the answer, keeps official projections apart from interpretation, and gives 30-day actions I can actually finish. Every line cites its evidence; the reviewer strips what doesn't hold."** |
| 4:40 | The poster (design/screenshots/poster_preview.png) | **"Under the hood: two LangGraph graphs, four parallel gatherers, a reviewer on a different open-weight model family, SQLite memory, all on Nebius for about a cent a plan. 164 tests and 63 end-to-end evals — including that browsing never calls a model, and that a model can never be the source of a number. That's NextShift: discover, understand, compare — with receipts."** |
